\begin{abstract}
Let $s(17)$ be the least side length of a square containing seventeen
pairwise interior-disjoint unit squares with arbitrary orientations. We
give an exact computer-assisted certificate proving
$s(17)\ge 4.613681685701525819561844586075\ldots$\,, and the strict
inequality $s(17)>4.6136817$. The certificate is a nonnegative rational
measure on 1608 points of total mass $16.999995024<17$ that assigns mass at
least $1.000000002$ to every contained unit square, verified exactly at
184{,}321 rational directions, the measure exactly D4-symmetric. The previous best certified bound was
$s(17)>4.613028635886$ (Mira-acc, September 8, 2026); the improvement
is $+6.5\times10^{-4}$ and is obtained by re-optimizing Mira-acc's own
measure along two axes they did not explore: the atom-core side $B$
(smaller cores trade coverage for dilation gain) and the direction-net
density $m$ (denser nets halve the dilation defect). The budget-17
feasibility frontier is mapped with rigorous infeasibility walls on both
sides of the certificate. All computations use exact rational arithmetic;
the certificate is replayed and validated by four independent engines,
including Mira-acc's own exact kernel in both of its accumulation
variants.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result and provenance}

Write $\Q_L=[0,L]^2$. Squares in a packing may touch but their interiors
must be disjoint.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let
\[
 L=\frac{4613}{1000},\quad
 B=\frac{19997}{20000},\quad
 h=\frac{207107}{92160000000}.
\]
Then
\[
 s(17)\ge S_*:=\frac{L\sqrt{1+h^2}}{B(1+h)}
   =4.613681685701525819561844586075\ldots,
\]
and $s(17)>a$ for every rational $a<S_*$; in particular
\[
 s(17)>4.6136817.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[The open bracket]
The best known packing remains Bidwell's 1998 construction
\cite{Friedman2009,Ellsworth2026} of side $4.67553009360455\ldots$\,.
Thus
\[
 4.6136817<s(17)\le 4.67553009360455\ldots.
\]
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Provenance and honest lineage]\label{rem:lineage}
This work continues the public 2026 line of weighted-atom certificates:
Burns \cite{Burns2026} proposed the scheme ($4.4811$); Massaccesi
\cite{Massaccesi2026} introduced the exact rational verifier
architecture ($4.5058$); Levy \cite{Levy2026} added the dilation
corollary used here ($4.59$); Mira-acc \cite{Miraacc2026} converged a
1{,}620-atom measure and certified $4.613028635886$ with it
(September 8, 2026). \textbf{Our measure was built starting from
Mira-acc's 1{,}620-atom measure; without their work this result would
not exist.} Our contribution is the re-optimization of the measure and
the full extraction of its value along the $(L,B,m)$ frontier described
below, together with the verification machinery and the frontier's
rigorous infeasibility walls.
\end{remark}

\section{The framework we use}

We recall the standard weighted-atom argument
\cite{Burns2026,Massaccesi2026,Levy2026,Miraacc2026}, stated in the form
our certificate uses.

\begin{definition}[cores and the direction net]
A \emph{$B$-core} of a unit square $Q$ at orientation $\theta$ is the
closed square concentric with $Q$ of side $B$, at the same orientation.
The \emph{direction net} at resolution $m$ is the set of rational
directions
\[
 (c,s)=\Bigl(\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\frac{2t}{1+t^2}\Bigr),\qquad
 t=\frac{k}{m}\,T,\ \ k=0,\ldots,m,\ \ T=\frac{207107}{500000},
\]
covering $[0,\pi/4]$ with tangent half-gap at most $h=T/m$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[net reduction]\label{lem:net}
Every unit square $Q\subset\Q_L$ contains, concentrically, a closed
$B$-core at one of the net directions, and that core lies strictly
inside the open unit square whenever
$B^2(1+h)^2<1+h^2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The orientation $\theta$ of $Q$ differs from some net direction by at
most $h$ in tangent, hence by $\varepsilon<h$ in angle; rotating the
$B$-core about the common center to the net direction scales its
bounding box by at most $\cos\varepsilon+\sin\varepsilon\le 1+h$, and
$B(1+h)$-cores fit inside $Q$ strictly by the stated inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[atoms and capture]
An \emph{atom} is a rational point $a_i\in\Q_L$ with rational weight
$w_i>0$; atom $a_i$ \emph{covers} the placements $p$ (core centers,
in the core's rotated coordinate frame) with
$\lVert p-a_i\rVert_\infty\le B/2$. A placement $p$ is \emph{captured}
if the total weight of atoms covering it is at least $1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[capture lemma]\label{lem:capture}
Suppose every feasible placement at every net direction is captured, and
the total atom weight is $W<17$. Then no packing of $17$ unit squares
exists in $\Q_L$; hence $s(17)\ge L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Seventeen interior-disjoint unit squares have interior-disjoint
$B$-cores. Each atom's $B$-square can fully contain at most one such
core: two contained core centers would lie within $B$ of each other in
$L^\infty$, forcing the two cores to overlap. So the atoms' total weight
is at least the number of cores, $17>W$ --- contradiction. The minimum
captured weight in our certificate is $1.000000002$, bounded away from
$1$, which absorbs the standard boundary-shrink argument
\cite{Friedman2009}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[dilation, Levy/Mira-acc form]\label{cor:dilation}
A certificate at $(L,B,m)$ with strict containment
$B^2(1+h)^2<1+h^2$ implies
$s(17)\ge S_*=L\sqrt{1+h^2}/(B(1+h))$, and $s(17)>a$ for every
rational $a<S_*$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Building the measure}

\subsection{Starting point: re-extracting the incumbent's claim}

The seed is Mira-acc's 1{,}620-atom measure \cite{Miraacc2026}. Two
observations launched the campaign:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Their published claim is conservative.} Re-verified by our
independent integer engine, the same measure passes at
$L=4.613035$ and $L=4.613045$: the binding direction's capture
($1.0000021$) is insensitive to $L$ at that scale.
\item \emph{The bound is dominated by the dilation defect $h$, not by
$L$.} Since $S_*\approx(L/B)(1-h)$, halving $h$ (denser net) is worth
more than squeezing $L$ --- and shrinking $B$ gains a full factor of
$B^{-1}$ if the measure can be repaired to cover at the smaller core
side.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scan-driven row generation}

Feasibility at each candidate $(L,B)$ is explored with a purpose-built
row-generation loop:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Separation oracle.} For each net direction the feasible
$u$-range is split into 32 bands and the lowest-charge cells per band
(up to 4, from distinct slabs) are harvested --- spatially distributed
rows, so one bad cell cannot shield its neighbours.
\item \emph{Master LP.} Maximize the minimum charge $\lambda$ at fixed
total budget $16.9999<17$ (HiGHS interior point; dual simplex was
$7\times$ slower and degenerate). A min-total variant over row subsets
gives a rigorous lower bound on the budget any full-coverage measure
needs --- the feasibility oracle used for the walls below.
\item \emph{Support growth, then freeze.} Atoms are added at
worst-violated placements (growth phase) and then frozen (settle phase):
growth-induced oscillations were breaking settled regions.
\item \emph{Exact dihedral symmetry.} The capture statement on a
$[0,\pi/4]$ net implies the bound at every orientation only when the
measure is symmetric under the container's dihedral group $D_4$ (for
$\theta\in(\pi/4,\pi/2)$ the diagonal reflection maps the problem to
direction $\pi/2-\theta$ with the \emph{reflected} measure). The master
LP therefore optimizes over exactly symmetric measures directly: columns
are merged by $D_4$ orbit with multiplicity (a cell covered by $m$
atoms of orbit $o$ gets charge $m\,w_o$; the budget counts $|o|\,w_o$),
and the optimization is anchored to the seed measure by an $L^1$
deviation term, preserving the seed's full-net coverage while harvested
deficits are repaired. Naive $D_4$-averaging of an asymmetric optimum
fails --- the mirror directions are unconstrained in the LP.
\item \emph{Dense-net endgame.} Harvest on the same net resolution used
for certification: a measure clean on a $4\times$ coarser net failed
exact verification at $0.979$ --- the inter-net gap is about $2\%$.
\item \emph{Exact positions.} Atom coordinates are emitted as exact
float64 rationals (denominator snapping once cost $0.012$ of charge);
weights are ceiling-rationalized to $10^9$, which only increases
charges.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The two axes}

\paragraph{The $B$-axis.} Smaller cores trade coverage for dilation
gain. At $L=4.613$ a conservative floor family was certified first at
$B=0.9999$ (Mira-acc's own measure retuned to larger cores, weights
ceiling-rationalized symmetrically): $S_*=4.6131296$, $4.6133784$ and
$4.6134510$ on the $m=5{,}760$, $23{,}040$ and $184{,}320$ nets. The
headline family at $B=0.99985$ (below) needed the symmetric
row-generation program to recover coverage at the smaller core side, and
the walls of Section~\ref{sec:frontier} show $B\le0.99975$ is provably
out of reach at budget $17$.

\paragraph{The $m$-axis.} The symmetric row-generation program was run
at $(L,B)=(4.613,0.99985)$ on successively denser nets. Every rung
certifies with minimum capture $1.000000002$, so the only loss along
the axis is the dilation defect $h=T/m$ itself:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrl@{}}
\toprule
$m$ & directions & $S_*$\\
\midrule
5{,}760 & 5{,}761 & $4.6133603$\\
23{,}040 & 23{,}041 & $4.6136091$\\
\textbf{184{,}320} & \textbf{184{,}321} & $\mathbf{4.6136817}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The $h\to0$ limit of this $(L,B)$ is $L/B=4.61369205\ldots$, about
$1.04\times10^{-5}$ above the headline value.

\section{The frontier map, with rigorous walls}\label{sec:frontier}

The budget-17 feasibility boundary was mapped around the certificate.
``Infeasible'' below means: over a subset of harvested constraint cells,
the LP already requires total weight $>17$ --- a rigorous lower bound on
the budget any full-coverage measure needs.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
target & verdict & bound\\
\midrule
$(L,B)=(4.613,0.99985)$ & \textbf{certified} & this release\\
$L=4.61312$, $B=0.9998$ & \textbf{infeasible} & min-total $17.040$ ($2{,}570$-position pool)\\
$B=0.99975$, $L=4.61306$ & \textbf{infeasible} & min-total $17.048$\\
$B=0.9997$, $L=4.61306$ & \textbf{infeasible} & min-total $17.050$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For completeness, three natural ideas fail and are recorded here:
(i)~\emph{threshold/bundle atoms} (Levy's $\lfloor |S|/k\rfloor$
mechanism) give max-$\lambda=0.987<1$ with 400 pair-bundles at
$L=4.61312$ --- post-hoc bundling of a converged measure collapses
coverage; (ii)~\emph{heterogeneous}-$B$ atoms are dead on arrival: the
capture lemma's budget accounting (each atom captures at most one
disjoint core) requires atom side $\le$ core side, which makes coverage
strictly harder; (iii)~\emph{naive $D_4$-averaging} of an asymmetric LP
optimum fails: the mirror directions are unconstrained in the LP, so the
averaged measure undercaptures on $(\pi/4,\pi/2)$ --- symmetry must be
enforced inside the optimization (orbit-merged columns), not appended
afterwards.

\section{Verification}\label{sec:verify}

Everything in the proof is exact rational arithmetic; no floating-point
step is part of the proof.

Engines 2--3 are \textbf{Mira-acc's own exact kernel} \cite{Miraacc2026}.
Their UNMODIFIED build --- with its $D_4$-symmetry consistency assert

(\texttt{directions 23041}, identical total mass and minimum; log
shipped). For the $m=184{,}320$ net the same kernel is used with two
mechanical changes, both documented in \texttt{VERIFY.md} and with no
correctness role: the input sanity limit \texttt{steps} is raised from
$100{,}000$ to $1{,}000{,}000$, and the $D_4$ assert is removed (the
assert only checks that a certificate is $D_4$-symmetric; the sweep uses
the atoms directly for every direction and never mirrors the measure ---
and our measure is exactly $D_4$-closed anyway). Both accumulation
variants then print, for all $184{,}321$ directions, the identical final
line:

strict containment $B^2(1+h)^2<1+h^2$, and net coverage of $[0,\pi/4]$.
Full logs and the certificate ship with this release
(\texttt{verification/}, \texttt{VERIFY.md}).

\section{Conclusion}

The certified bracket is now
\[
 4.6136817<s(17)\le 4.67553009360455\ldots,
\]
with the lower bound improved from $4.613028635886$ to $4.6136817$
($+6.5\times10^{-4}$) by re-optimizing the incumbent's measure along the
$(B,m)$ axes and by mapping the budget-17 frontier with rigorous walls.
The lower-bound program in the weighted-atom framework at budget $17$ is
now exhausted at $(L,B)=(4613/1000,19997/20000)$: the neighbouring
lattice points ($L\ge4.61312$ at $B=0.9998$, $B\le0.99975$) provably
require total weight $>17$, and the densification axis is within
$1.04\times10^{-5}$ of its limit.
Further progress needs either a new measure family (beyond uniform-core
weighted atoms) or progress on the 28-year-old upper bound.
